Can you tell a human essay from an AI one?

A look at 6,000 real student essays, labelled as written by a Human or an AI. I built simple text features and compared the two groups to see what really differs.

1. Humans write longer essays

Share of essays in each length range (%)

AI essays bunch up around 250 to 400 words. Human essays are spread out, with many running past 500.

2. Style habits differ

Average per essay, shown as % of the Human value

AI uses more commas and longer words, but shorter sentences. Humans write longer sentences with fewer commas.

3. Vocabulary variety overlaps a lot

Share of essays by unique-word ratio (%)

The two curves almost sit on top of each other. AI is only slightly higher, and part of that is because its essays are shorter.

4. Same topics, similar words

Top 10 words (stopwords removed), count of uses

Both groups talk about the same essay prompts. Humans lean on casual words like “just” and “like”.

What this means for decisions

  • No single clue is enough. Every feature overlaps between the groups, so judging an essay on length or commas alone would give false accusations.
  • Teachers: treat these patterns as a reason to look closer or talk to the student, not as proof.
  • Detector builders: combine several features (length, sentence length, commas, word length) rather than relying on one.
  • Watch the data: the sample is about 60% human, and both groups answer the same prompts, so topic words say little about style.
Data: DAIGT “LLM – Detect AI Generated Text” essays (Kaggle), random sample of 6,000 (seed 42), duplicates removed. Word length counts spaces, so it reads slightly high. Word-count bars group essays over 950 words into the last bar. Built with pandas and seaborn for the analysis; charts drawn with Chart.js.
